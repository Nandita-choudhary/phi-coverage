## Checked by hand (17 Oct 2026)

The auto-generated part above is a first pass. These notes record what was confirmed by
reading the sheets, and they win where the two disagree.

### Question 1: are people downgrading instead of leaving?

**Membership and Benefits June 2026.xlsx → `HTPoliciesXTypeofCover`**

- Two header rows: display names (Year, MonthEnd, State, PolicyCover, ExcessAmount, Value),
  then internal names (`Line_Item`, `Value_Calc`). Data starts on row 3.
- 7,272 data rows: 77 quarters from June 2007 to June 2026, 8 states, no total rows, so
  summing the states gives Australia.
- `PolicyCover`: Full cover · Reduced cover and some lifetime exclusions · Reduced cover but
  no lifetime exclusions · Some lifetime exclusions but no reduced cover.
- `ExcessAmount`: Nil · Up to $500 single · Over $500 single. The excess cap rose from 500 to
  750 dollars (single) on 1 April 2019, so this series breaks by rule there.
- Check figures, June quarters, Australia: 4,368,148 hospital policies in 2007 (63.6% full
  cover); 6,268,336 in 2026 (26.8% full cover).
- Known quirk: in the December 2021 quarter, every state's "Full cover, Nil" count ends in .1
  (197,506.1 in NSW). `load_apra.py` rounds those eight values and announces it on every run.

### Question 2: is the pool getting older?

**Membership Trends June 2026_0.xlsx → `MembershipByAgeData`**

- Five tables side by side, separated by empty columns, each with its own header depth:
  `AgeCohort_HT` (columns A–F, data from row 5), `AgeCohort_HST` (H–M, from row 4),
  `AgeCohort_CDMP` (O–T, from row 4), `AgeCohort_Ancillary` (V–AA, from row 4) and
  `HistoricalTrends_Ancillary` (AC–AH, from row 3, using `StateShortName` and `Value`).
- `AgeCohort_HT` is hospital cover: Year, MonthEnd, State, Gender, Age, InsuredPersons.
  24,240 rows: 77 quarters, 8 states, M and F, 20 age bands. Age is the band's start, 0 to
  95; check the glossary for whether 95 means 95 and over.
- Check figures, June quarters, Australia: 9,144,641 insured persons in 2007 (13.3% aged
  65+); 12,823,752 in 2026 (20.9% aged 65+).
